How this is worked out

Every second, this page replays today 4,000 times. Each replay shifts all 110 scheduled trains by a random delay, sprinkles in freight, drops the gate from 4 min before a through train to 1 min after (6 min for one that stops at Ambli Road), and keeps the gate down when the next train is due within 2 min of it reopening. The numbers above are just how often you were stuck, and for how long.

A train with a live delay is placed around that delay, give or take about 3 min — wider if it is badly late or the sighting is old, but never tighter, because a reported delay still has to survive the run to the crossing. Everything else falls back to its 7-day average, and the 90 trains with no recorded average draw one at random from the 20 that have one — which is why their arrival bars are so wide.

Freight is a guess. There is no public freight schedule, so the model assumes about 4 a day at 14 min each, 60% of them overnight — fitted to match observed waits, not measured. That is the weakest part of this.

Live delays come from railyatri.in, refreshed by running python web_data.py --live and pasting the result into this page. They are crowd-sourced, so treat a quiet train as unknown rather than on time.
