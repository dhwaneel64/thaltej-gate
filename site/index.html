How this is worked out

Your phone replays today 4,000 times every minute. Each replay shifts all 110 scheduled trains by a random delay, sprinkles in freight, drops the gate from 4 min before a through train to 1 min after (6 min for one stopping at Ambli Road), and holds it down when the next train is due within 2 min of it reopening. The figures are simply how often you were stuck, and for how long. Nothing is computed on a server.

A train with a live delay is placed around that delay, give or take about 3 min — wider if it is badly late or the sighting is old, never tighter, because a reported delay still has to survive the run to the crossing. Everything else falls back to its 7-day average, and the 90 trains with no recorded average draw one at random from the 20 that have one — which is why their arrival bars are so wide.

Freight is a guess. There is no public freight schedule, so the model assumes about 4 a day at 14 min each, 60% of them overnight — fitted to match observed waits, not measured. It is the weakest part of this, and at quiet moments it is the main thing closing the gate.

Live delays come from railyatri.in and refresh on their own roughly every 15 minutes. A train whose run has not started yet has no live delay and keeps its average, so late-night crossings are mostly modelled rather than observed.
